# Three Theorems

These are early working principles for the Axis Thought Packet project.

They are not presented as mathematical proofs in v0.1. They are design theorems: compact claims that guide the specification.

## 1. Continuity Theorem

A thought can continue across time if its state, intent, constraints, and unresolved direction are preserved with enough structure.

Implication:

An agent does not need the full raw history to continue useful work. It needs a faithful packet of the active thought state.

## 2. Translation Theorem

A thought can move between agents when its essential axes are translated into the target agent's usable context.

Implication:

Handoff is not only copying text. It is translating focus, assumptions, and next actions into a form that the next participant can act on.

## 3. Inheritance Theorem

A later thought state can inherit useful structure from an earlier state without preserving every private or incidental detail.

Implication:

Public research artifacts can inherit from private exploration while removing private material and retaining the reusable conceptual structure.
